2. HIPAA Role

When Cardio AI processes protected health information (PHI) for health systems and payers, it acts as a business associate of those covered entities. It must then sign a BAA with each customer, comply with the HIPAA Security Rule and the applicable parts of the Privacy Rule, and pass the same obligations to any subcontractor that handles PHI.

The consumer mobile app may fall outside HIPAA when a consumer uses it independently of a provider. In that case, the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule and state consumer health privacy laws can apply. Once a patient connects the app to their provider through IoMT-RPM, data handled on the provider’s behalf is treated as PHI. [Counsel to confirm the app’s status and required notices.]

4. HIPAA Security Rule Safeguards

Administrative

  • Designated security officer and privacy officer: TBD
  • Documented HIPAA risk analysis and risk management plan, completed before pilots and updated annually.
  • Workforce security training at hire and annually; sanctions policy.
  • Incident response and breach notification procedures (notification to customers within the BAA deadline).
  • Contingency plan: backups, disaster recovery and emergency operations.
  • Vendor management: BAAs with every subprocessor that handles PHI.

Technical

  • Access control: unique user IDs; organization-scoped RBAC (live); least privilege; no multi-factor authentication implemented today (open item); automatic session timeout (12-hour JWT expiry; no refresh token or idle-timeout mechanism).
  • Encryption: TLS for data in transit — terminated at the hosting provider’s edge (Render) for inbound traffic; this app’s own outbound calls (IoMT client) pin a TLS 1.2 minimum in code. Encryption at rest: Data Room documents are encrypted at the application layer (Fernet/AES) before they reach either storage backend, independent of provider; Postgres (users, organizations, audit log — no PHI stored there) relies on Render’s managed-Postgres disk encryption.
  • Audit controls: logging of both user actions (login, logout, signup, approval/rejection/disabling) and pipeline-task outcomes to a persisted, queryable Postgres table (auth/audit.py, GET /api/audit/events, admin-only); retained 400 days by default and purged on a daily scheduled job (render.yaml). The in-memory, 200-event agent-hop trace remains in addition, for the live dashboard only.
  • Integrity: validation of inbound files (e.g., X12) and protection against unauthorized change.
  • Transmission security: encrypted APIs and integrations only.

Physical

No company-operated data centers; physical safeguards are provided by cloud providers under their certifications. Company laptops use full-disk encryption, screen locks and endpoint protection (not verifiable from the product codebase — confirm against IT/workforce security policy).

  • Tenant isolation by organization at the application and database layers.
  • Separate development, staging and production environments; no PHI outside production: not yet true today — render.yaml defines a single production environment with no separate staging/dev blueprint (open item before pilots).
  • Confirmed — secrets (JWT signing key, R2 credentials, data room password) are read from environment variables; Render auto-generates/stores them and they are never committed to the repo; no hardcoded secrets or committed .env files found in the codebase.
  • Not yet in place — no CI/CD pipeline or dependency/vulnerability scanning found in the codebase; no penetration test has been commissioned (open item before pilots).
  • Not independently configured in the codebase — relies on Render-managed PostgreSQL’s platform backup capability; restore testing has not yet been performed (open item).
  • Not yet in place — no security event logging/alerting tooling found in the codebase (open item).
