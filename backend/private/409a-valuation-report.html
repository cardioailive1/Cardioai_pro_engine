1. Executive Summary

The fair market value of Cardio AI Corp Common Stock as of September 18th, 2026 is estimated at $0.75 per share. The value is a weighted conclusion of four approaches: two market approaches (backsolve to Mayo Clinic Platform’s $800,000 SAFE, signed and effective October 5th, 2026, and guideline public companies), the income approach (risk-adjusted forecast from Revenue Model V7) and the cost approach (replacement cost of the Company’s technology). Each equity value is allocated to common stock with an Option Pricing Model (OPM), and a 30% discount for lack of marketability (DLOM) is applied.

That value is measured directly by the income approach (what the technology is forecast to earn) and the cost approach (what it would cost to rebuild, about $10.5 million), and is reflected in the price agreed by Mayo Clinic Platform (Section 10).

2. Purpose, Scope and Standard of Value

This report estimates the fair market value of the Company’s Common Stock to support the exercise price of stock options granted under the Cardio AI Corp 2026 Equity Incentive Plan, in line with Section 409A and Treasury Regulation §1.409A-1(b)(5)(iv), and to support stock-based compensation accounting under ASC 718.

Fair market value is the price at which property would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, neither under compulsion, nor with reasonable knowledge of relevant facts. The methods follow the AICPA Accounting and Valuation Guide, Valuation of Privately-Held-Company Equity Securities Issued as Compensation.

Information relied on: Cardio AI Corp cap table V3 as updated by management; management’s statements on the Mayo Clinic SAFE (amount, 20% discount, $50 million post-money valuation cap) and the status of the pre-seed round; and Lin X, et al., Front. Cardiovasc. Med. 2022;9:903660.

3. Company Overview

Cardio AI Corp is an early-stage, pre-revenue company developing artificial-intelligence products across four areas: risk prediction of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) within 30–90 days; payer analytics for insurance companies; a consumer-facing iOS and Android app for remote patient monitoring (RPM); and echocardiogram analysis to detect early-stage heart failure (HFrEF and HFpEF) and regional wall motion abnormalities, a sign of myocardial infarction, and to measure cardiac function, including on portable bedside ultrasound. Its management includes Sampson Kontomah, Founder and Chief Executive Officer, Everlyn Ndirangu, Chief Financial Officer, Galax Womack, Chief Technology Officer, Avi Patel, VP Engineering.

Advisory Board Members: Dr. Tamanna Nahar, Co-founder & Chief Medical Advisor, Dr. Dominic Merante, Oleg Feldgajer

Technology developed and deployed

Cardio AI is a technology company: its value rests almost entirely on the technology the founder developed and deployed from 2023 to 2026, personally funding all development, subscription and deployment costs. That technology was contributed to the Company in exchange for the founder’s shares on August 10th, 2026 under a Technology and IP Assignment, and Contribution Agreement. It demonstrates what this class of technology can achieve rather than the Company’s own results. Using 6,953 examinations across two hospitals, the authors built a fully automated pipeline for view selection, quality control, segmentation, wall-motion detection and ejection-fraction measurement.

Competitive landscape

Management’s competitive matrix (January 2026) compares Cardio AI with ten cardiac AI companies: Eko Health, Cleerly, Ultromics, Cardiologs, Viz.ai, Arterys, iCardio.ai, Idoven, AliveCor and Tempus. Most competitors cover one or two use cases (ECG, a single imaging modality, or remote monitoring). Cardio AI’s technology spans ECG analysis, imaging, remote monitoring, treatment support and five CVD risk calculators.

Competitors have raised substantial capital to build narrower products, for example Eko Health ($165 million), Cleerly ($223 million), Viz.ai ($250 million) and AliveCor ($200 million), per the matrix. This supports the view that comparable technology is costly to build. Cardiologs (acquired by Philips) and Arterys (acquired by Tempus) show strategic buyers acquiring cardiac AI. The matrix’s unit-economics and return projections are management estimates and are not relied on in this valuation.

Stage and risk

The Company has no revenue and no FDA clearance. Its commercial traction is three signed LOIs and structured customer and pricing research (Section 6). The four product lines broaden the addressable market (providers, payers and consumers). Key risks are regulatory (the echocardiogram and MACE-prediction tools are likely to be regulated as software as a medical device, requiring 510(k) or De Novo clearance, while payer analytics may not be), clinical validation, reimbursement, data privacy (HIPAA) for the consumer app, competition from larger imaging-AI and analytics vendors, and financing. These risks support a high volatility and a substantial marketability discount.

6. Traction: Market and Business Model Validation

Management reports the following commercial evidence which has been independently verified; supporting documents are listed in Section 12.

  • Customer discovery: 65 structured interviews (February–August 2026): 16 enterprise health systems, 22 custom deployments (large health systems and cardiology groups) and 27 payers. The top validated pain point is diagnostic fragmentation and liability exposure from missed diagnoses.
  • Letters of intent: 3 signed nonbinding LOIs from TMC Health (Tucson, AZ; $1.3B revenue), Erlanger Health System (Chattanooga, TN; $1.2B) and AtlantiCare Health System (Atlantic City, NJ; $1.2B), each first met in discovery interviews (#9, #11 and #12). The discovery analysis estimates realistic Year 1 ARR of $6.2M–$8.4M: these 3 LOIs plus 5–8 more enterprise contracts from the named prospect list at about $800K per year. Revenue Model V7 projects 15 Year 1 contracts, supported by 4 enterprise sales reps.
  • Named pipeline: the remaining 35 enterprise and custom prospects and 27 payers from the discovery interviews are staged by their documented outcomes. The 10 prospects at proposal or pilot-agreed stage (Valley Health, Centra Health, Inspira Health, Mercyhealth, Palomar Health, CoxHealth, Hospital Sisters Health System, ProMedica, HeartPlace and PIMA Heart and Vascular) are the source of the additional Year 1 contracts.
  • Pricing research: a Van Westendorp price sensitivity analysis with 32 cardiologists found an acceptable range of $60–$110 PMPM for the enterprise platform; 78% would displace a current point solution; CFOs validated ROI breakeven at a 3–4% readmission reduction.
  • Model performance: 96.8% diagnostic accuracy on an internal multi-modal cardiovascular dataset (ECG, echocardiogram and CT), with an algorithmic bias gap below 1.8 percentage points across race and gender subgroups, which management states exceeds FDA 2024 digital health equity guidance.
  • Comparable transactions: Octagos Health ($43M Series B) and Viz.ai enterprise contracts; management states the 96.8% accuracy supports an $80 PMPM enterprise willingness-to-pay benchmark.
  • Payer economics: $10–$12 PMPM payer pricing validated against Medicare Advantage actuarial models showing $35,000–$75,000 of medical cost avoided per prevented cardiac event, an 8–15× ROI on the PMPM cost for payer partners.

Effect on the valuation

This evidence strengthens the case that the Company has a validated market need and a credible path to revenue. It is reflected in the selection of a DLOM at the low end of the range, 30% rather than 35% (Section 9), which raises the concluded value by about $0.05 per share. The effect is limited to the discount because the backsolve already reflects what Mayo Clinic Platform knew when it invested, the LOIs are nonbinding and the additional Year 1 contracts are not yet signed, and the 96.8% accuracy is on an internal dataset that has not been cleared by the FDA or peer reviewed.

The 30% DLOM relies on this evidence being accurate.

The backsolve finds the total equity value at which the SAFE’s as-converted shares are worth their $1.60 effective price. The OPM models each class as a call option on company value, so the SAFE’s liquidation preference and the common’s upside are priced consistently. The unallocated pool (1,074,239 shares) is excluded. The granted options (2,034,457 at $1.00, about 13% of fully diluted shares) are modeled as a separate class that shares in value above $1.00 per share, and both SAFEs ($830,000) are included.

Because Cardio AI’s value rests on its technology, three of the four approaches value the technology directly: the income approach through the revenue it is forecast to earn, the guideline public company method through what public markets pay for comparable cardiac AI revenue, and the cost approach through what it would cost to rebuild. The SAFE backsolve captures what an informed investor implied. The four indications range from $10.5 million to $22.8 million, and the weighted equity value of $15.7 million is above the $10.5 million cost to rebuild the technology.

9. Discount for Lack of Marketability

Common holders have no market for their shares, face a multi-year wait for liquidity and rank behind the SAFE. Put-option models (Finnerty, Chaffe) at 70% volatility over three years, and restricted-stock studies, commonly indicate 30–40% for early-stage companies. A 30% DLOM is selected, at the low end of the range. Pre-revenue, pre-clearance risk is offset by credible published evidence for the technology, a strategic investor (Mayo Clinic Platform), three signed LOIs and structured customer and pricing validation (Section 6), which together shorten the expected path to a liquidity event.

Income approach: risk-adjusted forecast

The income approach uses Revenue Model V7 (FY2027–FY2029 revenue of $8.7 million, $85.0 million and $171.6 million, including payer analytics repriced by line of business and the complementary MedCRM and IoMT-RPM products; 15 enterprise contracts projected in Year 1, of which 8 are realistic from the LOIs and named pipeline). FY2029 revenue in each scenario is converted to an exit value at a 5.0× revenue multiple, discounted to the valuation date at a 60% venture rate over 3.2 years, and reduced by 50% for dilution from the future financing the plan requires.
